Welcome! I am SoHee Park, an incoming Ph.D. student in the Department of Government at the University of Texas at Austin. I study comparative politics, with a focus on political institutions, both formal and informal. The regional focus of my research is East Asia, with particular attention to South Korea, China/Taiwan, and Japan.

I hold an M.A. in Political Science and a B.A. in Political Science from Ewha Womans University, where I was in the Scranton Honors Program and minored in Women's Studies. I am a Fulbright Graduate Study Award recipient for 2026–2028.

Working Papers

  1. Revisiting Informal Politics in East Asia: The Institutional Dimension of Factionalism. Submitted to Journal of East Asian Studies [R&R]
    Abstract

    This paper develops an institutionalist argument of factional stability to explain why political factions exhibit divergent levels of durability across East Asian democracies and autocracies. Existing explanations emphasize political structures, cultural norms, or strategic elite behavior, but each captures only part of the puzzle. I argue that factional stability depends on the degree of factional institutionalization, defined by the interaction of two mechanisms: intra-factional monitoring and inter-factional coordination. When these mechanisms reinforce one another, factions achieve organizational consistency, enabling them to withstand political change. A comparative analysis of Japan, South Korea, China, and Taiwan (1949-1990) demonstrates how these mechanisms operate differently across regime types. The study contributes to debates on informal institutions, elite politics, and regime dynamics by identifying the institutional foundations of factional stability across political regimes.
